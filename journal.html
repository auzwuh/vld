JOURNAL

Dated entries for Robotics Foundations I: what I worked on, what was hard, and what I'll do next.

Week 11 November 10

1. What did I work on today?

I studied orientation error for inverse kinematics algorithms. The lesson covered three ways to write it: Euler angles, angle and axis, and unit quaternions.

2. What was challenging, and how did I handle it?

Position error is a simple subtraction (e_P = p_d − p_e), but orientation error can't be done that way. The Euler angle method needs inverse formulas to get φ_e from R_e. The angle and axis method gives a shorter formula, e_O = r sin θ. I wrote the three methods side by side and noted what each one needs.

3. How focused was I today? (1–4)

3. I read through all three methods and wrote them out. I did not work an example with numbers.

4. What will I do differently or keep doing next session?

Work one orientation error example with the angle and axis method. Then check how the unit quaternion method differs.

Week 9 November 3

1. What did I work on today?

I studied inverse kinematics algorithms. These use the Jacobian to compute joint angles for a target tip position, one step at a time.

2. What was challenging, and how did I handle it?

I didn't understand why the Jacobian inverse method fails near a singularity. I connected it to the singularity lesson: det(J) goes to 0 there, so J can't be inverted. I wrote that next to the algorithm steps.

3. How focused was I today? (1–4)

3. I started on my own and finished the lesson. I did not run through an example by hand.

4. What will I do differently or keep doing next session?

Work one inverse kinematics example by hand on the two-link arm. Then start the equations of motion section.

Week 8 October 20

1. What did I work on today?

I studied redundancy in the Jacobian section. A robot is redundant when it has more joints than it needs for the task.

2. What was challenging, and how did I handle it?

I didn't get how a redundant arm can have many joint solutions for one tip position. I drew a planar arm with three links and moved the elbow while keeping the tip fixed. That showed me the extra joint gives the arm extra freedom.

3. How focused was I today? (1–4)

3. I started on my own and finished the lesson. I did not do a practice problem.

4. What will I do differently or keep doing next session?

Do one practice problem on redundancy. Then start the statics section.

Week 7 October 13

1. What did I work on today?

I worked on singularities in the Jacobian section. I looked at a two-link planar arm and how it reaches a singularity when the second joint angle is 0 or π.

2. What was challenging, and how did I handle it?

I didn't get why det(J) = a₁a₂s₂ meant the arm loses movement. I redrew the arm at θ₂ = 0 and saw that both links line up, so the tip can't move along the arm's length. That showed me why the two velocity vectors become parallel.

3. How focused was I today? (1–4)

3. I started on my own and finished the lesson. I did not try a practice problem afterward.

4. What will I do differently or keep doing next session?

Work through one practice problem on singularities before moving on. Keep drawing the arm whenever the equations don't make sense.
